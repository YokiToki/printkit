# Design

## Context

The baseplate splitter (`geometry/split.ts`, `connectors.ts`) is grid-aware: cuts are between cells, connectors go into ribs. A general model has no ribs; connectors must be placed in the *section* of the cut. `41` supplies stack operations and booleans, `42` supplies planar region picking. Slicers (Cut tool with connectors), Meshmixer (Plane Cut, Separate Shells) and Fusion (Split Body, holes) are the references.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals**: reliable cuts of closed solids, parts that print and assemble (clearances, depth limits), a hole tool with real fastener presets, editable and derived results.
**Non-Goals**: topology-aware smart cutting, threads, wrapping text.

## Decisions

1. **Derived parts.** `doc.cuts: CutGroup[]` where `CutGroup = {id, sourceObject, planes: Plane[] | grid {x[], y[], z[]}, connectors: ConnectorSet, bed?: {…}}`. Each resulting part is an ordinary object with `source: {kind:'cutPart', group, index}` (and its own transform = identity relative to the source frame, so the parts stay assembled in the scene by default, with an *Explode* slider like the baseplate's exploded view). The source object is hidden and listed under the group in the outliner; deleting the group restores the source. Editing the group recomputes all parts; parts may carry their own stack after the cut (`41`), applied to their derived solid.
2. **Cutting.** `cutSolid(solid, group)` splits with `Manifold.splitByPlane` (or `trimByPlane` for keep-one-side) for arbitrary planes; the grid variant is a sequence of axis splits ordered x, y, z. Requires a closed solid (guard from `41`). Both halves are closed by construction. Parts are labelled by column letter and row number as in `split.ts`; arbitrary single planes label A and B.
3. **Split to bed.** `autoCuts(size, usable)` per axis: fewest parts (`ceil(size / usable)`), cuts spread evenly, optionally shifted by a tolerance to align with section minima (sections with the smallest area are preferred within ±10 % of the evenly spaced position) — a cheap way to avoid cutting through large solid areas. The printer, bed and margin come from the export settings through `usableBed`. Parts bigger than the bed after the cut (diagonal parts of rotated objects are not rotated automatically) are marked `oversize` like the baseplate's.
4. **Section and placement.** The cut section is `CrossSection` from the solid sliced at the plane (`Manifold.slice`), expressed in plane coordinates. `placeConnectors(section, {kind, count, size, margin})` finds candidate positions on a grid whose cell is the connector pitch, keeps those whose clearance disc lies inside the section inset by `margin` (default connector diameter), chooses `count` positions by farthest-point sampling starting from the section's pole of inaccessibility (so pairs of pegs spread, singles go to the centre), and returns them in order. The user can drag, add and delete in a 2D preview of the section; manual positions are stored in plane coordinates on the cut definition so they survive edits of the source as long as they stay inside (otherwise warning `cut.connector.outside`, connector skipped).
5. **Connector solids.** Peg: cylinder of diameter *d* and length *l* on part A (union), socket of diameter *d + clearance* and depth *l + 0.3* on part B (difference), both chamfered at the tip. Pin hole: socket in both parts for a separate pin (diameter from the pin presets). Magnet pocket: in both parts, diameter *d + tolerance*, depth *h + tolerance* (polarity left to the user). Dovetail: a trapezoid profile (length, angle 15°, depth) extruded along the section polygon's chosen axis, key on A, socket on B grown by the clearance, depth limited by the material thickness. Depth for all is limited to the minimum of the configured depth and the available material behind the cut at that point minus 1.5 mm (ray distance through the solid along the normal); less than 2 mm available skips the connector with `cut.connector.thin`. Presets and clearances come from a shared `connectorSpec.ts` extracted from `connectors.ts` so the baseplate keeps its values.
6. **Collision checks.** After building, each connector solid is intersected with the part to verify it is fully inside (volume of connector ∩ part ≥ 99 % of the connector volume for the pocket, ≥ 99 % of the peg inside the opposite part's socket envelope); failures give `cut.connector.outside` or `cut.connector.overlap` with the connector index, shown in the preview in red.
7. **Hole tool.** Surface picking returns `(point, normal)` from the mesh raycaster (`42`); the hole op stores `{point, normal, up}` in object-local space plus parameters `{kind: 'simple'|'counterbore'|'countersink'|'magnet'|'insert', d, depth|through, cbD, cbDepth, csAngle, tolerance, preset?}`. The tool solid is a stack of cylinders/cones along the normal, starting slightly above the surface (+0.2 mm) and subtracted as a `boolean(subtract, generated tool)` in the evaluator (a generated tool is a solid, not a scene object). Presets: M2–M5 screw clearance (loose/normal), heads (DIN 912 and ISO 7380 sizes), countersink 90°, brass heat inserts M2–M5 (outer diameter and length table, documented from vendor ranges), magnets from the `06a` list plus common sizes (6×2, 8×3, 10×2); the table is data in `geometry/spec.ts` style, hand-written and cited in README. `through` extends along the normal to leave the solid (bbox diagonal). Offsets: numeric position fields move the point along the picked face's plane (face frame) relative to the object origin or to a picked edge.
8. **Text on a face.** `text` op: `{point, normal, up, text, font, size, spacing, align, mode: 'emboss'|'engrave', height|depth, rotation}`. The glyph outline polygons come from the rasteriser and `traceAlpha` outline code of Keychain, moved to a shared module `geometry/textShapes.ts`; polygons become a `CrossSection` (EvenOdd), extruded along the normal (emboss starts 0.05 mm inside the surface so the union is watertight; engrave starts 0.05 mm above), positioned by the frame. Only flat projection: on curved surfaces the text keeps its flat shape and the op reports `text.warning.curved` when the sampled surface deviates more than the text depth over the text extent. Fonts register via the existing lazy `FontFace` loaders.
9. **Viewport.** A cutting-plane widget (translucent quad with the gizmo of `39` attached, snaps to axes), cut section preview as a 2D inset panel, surface picking marker with the normal, ghosting of the hidden source. No new three.js dependency.
10. **History and persistence.** All edits go through `dispatch` with merging; `cuts` is a new optional field of the scene document (schema stays 1); normalisation drops connectors with missing groups.
11. **Export.** Derived parts export as ordinary parts with unique names `Name A1`; connectors that are loose pins (pin kind) are exported as separate small parts when "Include pins" is on (default on).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **Connector placement quality** on odd sections (thin, branched) is heuristic; the preview and manual edit are the escape, and unplaceable connectors are reported rather than forced.
- **Dovetail on arbitrary sections** is only reliable on sections with a straight run of sufficient length; otherwise it is refused with the reason, and pegs are suggested.
- **Hole presets** are hand-written tables; wrong sizes would be annoying, so values are conservative, cited, and editable (custom values always possible).
- **Derived parts and stack ops** interplay: operations added to a part apply to the derived solid after the cut; editing the cut can invalidate them (they fail visibly, as everywhere in `41`).
- **Text quality** depends on font tracing; very small text or thin strokes warn using the same check as Keychain (`textResolution` logic).

## Open Questions

- Whether pegs should be part of A (union) or loose pins by default (assumed: integrated pegs by default, loose pins as an option).
- Whether to show the exploded gap slider in the editor viewport for all groups (assumed yes, per group).
