# Design

## Context

The kernel is a triangle mesh (`manifold-3d`). Faces and edges of a B-rep do not exist, but what users call a face or an edge can be recognised: a flat region, a smooth patch, a chain of sharp edges. `42` already provides `planarRegion`. The risk, noted in `EDITOR.md`, is the "topological naming problem": references to recognised elements break when the model changes. The approach here: references describe *geometry* (a point, a normal, a kind and the region's approximate extent) and are re-resolved against the current mesh each time; ambiguity or absence is an explicit failure.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals**: faces and edges you can select with the mouse on any mesh; Push/Pull, Fillet and Chamfer for the common planar/circular cases; sketch on face; references that survive reasonable edits.
**Non-Goals**: a full B-rep, complete fillet theory, constrained face relations.

## Decisions

1. **Recognition (`topology/faces.ts`).** Two levels on the cached adjacency structure of the mesh.
   *Flat regions* are components of triangles joined across edges with a dihedral angle ≤ 1° (coplanar); *smooth groups* are components joined across edges with a dihedral angle ≤ 30°.
   A smooth group that is exactly one flat region stays flat. Otherwise the group's points are fitted to a primitive: cylinder (axis from the covariance of the triangle normals, radius and taper by least squares), cone (the same fit with a significant taper) and only then sphere — a cylinder's two vertex rings accidentally fit a sphere of radius √2·R, so the sphere check must come last. A good fit makes the whole group one face (`cylinder`/`cone`/`sphere`); without it the group falls back to its flat regions plus free-form patches. This keeps a faceted cylinder (64 segments) as one cylindrical face while a chamfered cube keeps its chamfer face separate from the top face.
   Result: `FaceRegion {id, tris, kind: 'planar'|'cylinder'|'cone'|'sphere'|'smooth', normal?, planeD?, axis?, radius?, apex?, area, centroid, loops, residual}`. Segmentation of the whole mesh is computed once per mesh version in the worker and cached by mesh hash; picking uses the region id per triangle.
   Measured: box → 6 planar regions, cylinder (64) → 2 planar caps + 1 cylindrical side (r = 10.000), sphere → 1 spherical region (r = 10.0), L bracket → all planar with exactly one concave edge.
2. **Edges (`topology/edges.ts`).** Feature edges are mesh edges whose dihedral angle exceeds the threshold (default 30°). Chains join feature edges through vertices where exactly two feature edges meet (a corner with three feature edges ends the chain — a cube therefore has 12 chains, a cylinder rim one closed circle). The degree test must ignore already consumed edges, otherwise neighbouring chains merge at a cube corner.
   A chain is classified `line` (best-fit line, max deviation ≤ 0.02 mm and no turn above 0.5°), `circle` (circle fit residual ≤ 0.02 mm and an arc span ≥ 15°) or `free`. A circle needs **at least four points** and a **consistent turn sign**: any three points fit a circle perfectly, so a triple proves nothing, and a wavy outline alternates its turn direction. Both conditions together keep a wave from passing as a shallow arc — without them a 20 mm wavy contour was cut into two "circles" of a fictitious 21 mm radius. A chain that fits neither is split greedily into straight runs (line tolerance 0.002 mm) and circular runs (circle tolerance 0.02 mm, span ≥ 15°, same consistency rule), so a rounded rectangle yields separate straight edges and arcs. Convexity is decided locally: the third vertex of one adjacent triangle must lie on the material side of the other triangle's normal; for a concave edge it does not. Edge ids are per mesh version.
   Measured: box → 12 straight convex edges, cylinder → 2 circles (r = 10) plus the seam handled as smooth, rounded rectangle → 8 straight and 8+ circular chains, noisy outline → no circle, wavy outline → one `free` chain.
3. **References (`topology/reference.ts`).** `FaceRef {kind:'face', point, normal, extent}` and `EdgeRef {kind:'edge', shape: line|circle|free, p0, p1 | centre, axis, radius | points}` in object-local space. `resolveFace(mesh, ref)` finds the region whose plane contains `point` within tolerance and whose normal matches within 5°, preferring the region containing the point; if two or none qualify the result is `ambiguous`/`missing`. `resolveEdge` similarly by geometry: a circle by centre, radius and axis, a line by the distance from the reference midpoint to the chain's line, a `free` chain by the pointwise distance of its polyline. The tolerance scales with the bounding box (tight 0.02 mm up to 1e-4 × diagonal). Operations store refs; evaluation resolves them against the solid *before* the operation, so earlier edits are accounted for.
4. **Push / Pull (`ops/pushPull.ts`).** For a planar region with outline loops projected to its plane: build `CrossSection` polygons (outer loops with holes, EvenOdd), extrude along the normal by *d* (`Manifold.extrude`, positive) and union with the body; for negative *d*, subtract the same prism extruded inwards. Faces whose region is not planar are refused (`face.error.notPlanar`). The prism overlaps the body by 0.01 mm to guarantee a watertight union. Multiple regions with the same plane and normal are processed together. A negative distance larger than the available material is clamped and reports the limit.
5. **Edge chamfer / fillet (`ops/edgeBreak3d.ts`).** The cross-section lives in the plane perpendicular to the edge and is built from the two adjacent faces: the direction along each face away from the edge (`v0`, `v1`) is `cross(edge, faceNormal)` with the sign taken from the third vertex of the adjacent triangle; for a cylindrical face it is the axial direction.
   - *chamfer*: the triangle `O, O + d·v0, O + d·v1` — the distance `d` is measured along the faces. Convex edges subtract the tool, concave edges add it.
   - *fillet*: the arc of radius `r` tangent to both faces. The centre is `C = O ∓ r·(n0 + n1)` (sign by convexity), the tangent points are `C ± r·n_i`, and the arc runs from `T0` to `T1` through the point closest to `O`. The arc is approximated by a polygon; the segment count follows the sagitta: `ceil(θ / (2·acos(1 − sagitta/r)))`, clamped to 4…48.
   - *straight chain*: the profile is extruded along the edge into a prism and placed by the frame (edge direction = local z, `v0` = local x).
   - *circular chain*: the same profile in the (radius, axial) plane is revolved about the fitted axis (`Manifold.revolve`).
   - *mixed chains*: straight runs become prisms, circular runs become revolves; the tools are unioned and overlap by 0.01 mm at the joints.
   Measured (spike, 20 mm cube): chamfer 2 mm on one top edge → volume 7960.000 mm³ (exactly 8000 − 2·2/2·20); fillet r = 3 mm → the removed profile area 1.9374 mm² against the analytic 1.9314 mm² (0.3 %), the volume 7961.25 against 7961.37 mm³. With the sagitta of the original design (0.02 mm) the error would be 3 %, so the sagitta is 0.002 mm and the segment limit 48. Chamfer 1 mm on the rim of a Ø20 cylinder → the top face measures Ø18 (slice area 254.1 against π·81 = 254.5 for a 64-gon). The four top edges of a cube chamfered or filleted together stay a valid closed solid (genus 0). A concave edge (the inner corner of an L bracket) gains exactly the triangle volume (+20 mm³ for d = 2 over a 10 mm edge).
   Ends of a chain are cut square (the prism spans the chain exactly), so where three edges meet the bands meet without a mitre — a visible approximation, documented in the UI help text.
   Limits: size ≤ 0.5 × the shorter adjacent face extent measured across the edge (otherwise `edge.error.tooLarge` with the maximum), dihedral between 20° and 160° (outside is refused with the reason), adjacent faces must be planar or cylindrical.
6. **Sketch on face.** The sketch plane gets a second kind `{face: FaceRef, frame}`: origin at the picked point projected on the face, z = face normal, x = direction of the longest straight boundary edge (fallback: the projection of the world X onto the plane, then world Y). `frame` caches the last resolved frame; `resolveFace` is retried when the body changes; if it fails, the cached frame is used and the sketch is flagged `detached` (the user can re-pick the face). Projected geometry: boundary loops become lines (straight chains) and arcs/circles (circular chains) with coincident points; `construction` or real by the user's choice; projected geometry is *not* kept in sync with later changes (explicit refresh button), which avoids hidden dependencies.
7. **Highlight and picking in the viewport.** Selection state `{mode, ids}` lives in the editor state; region membership is rendered through a per-triangle colour attribute (hover/selected) on a lightweight overlay mesh drawn with polygon offset, edges as line segments; picking resolves the triangle under the cursor to a region/edge id through the cached segmentation (ray against mesh with the BVH from `42`; edge picking by screen distance to chain polylines, 6 px).
8. **Performance.** Measured on a geodesic sphere (the worst case — one huge smooth region): 8 192 triangles → 23 ms, 32 768 → 47 ms, 131 072 → 130 ms, 524 288 → 520 ms. The Face/Edge modes are therefore available for meshes up to 1.5 M triangles (≈1.5 s) with a clear message above that limit; results are cached by mesh hash and shared by hover, selection and operations. The recognition runs in the worker with a progress job.
9. **Stack integration.** `pushPull {faces: FaceRef[], distance}`, `edgeBreak {edges: EdgeRef[], kind: chamfer|fillet, size}` are `41` stack operations: editable, reorderable, suppressible; failed refs fail the operation with `face.error.missing|ambiguous` / `edge.error.*` and the reason.
10. **Selection state is not history**, but the operations that use it are. Escape clears; Delete in Face mode is not defined (out of scope).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **Reference stability** is the weakest point: geometry-based references fail after large edits. Mitigation: failures are explicit and local to one operation; re-picking is one click; tolerances scale with the model; this is documented in the user-visible messages.
- **Fillet visual quality**: faceted arcs and approximate corners. Segment count follows a tolerance; export quality matters more than the on-screen preview, so the preview is coarser and the export rebuild uses the fine setting.
- **Segmentation of noisy scans** produces many tiny regions; the UI suggests Smooth/Decimate from `42` and offers a larger angle threshold.
- **Watertightness of unions** relies on small overlaps; covered by validity tests on every tool shape.
- **Scope creep** toward a general modelling kernel: operations are limited to what the recognised geometry supports and refuse the rest with a reason.
